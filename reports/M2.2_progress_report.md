# M2.2 progress report: fraud detector, core development

The baseline is the model committed at the end of J1 (`d3eae82`): XGBoost on engineered features, J1 hyper-parameters, decision threshold chosen by max F1 on the validation set. Everything below is measured against it. Numbers come from the saved notebooks and the CSVs in `reports/m2_2/`.

## Where things stand

- **No measurable gain on the headline metric, and there was little room for one.** The baseline scores PR-AUC 0.9972 on the test set (415,562 rows): 4 of 1,232 frauds missed, 0 false alarms. Re-fitting with five different seeds moves validation PR-AUC by 0.00026 (one standard deviation), so a change had to beat 0.00052 to count.
- **Three iterations, all logged** (`reports/m2_2/experiment_log.csv`). Two were reverted on validation. The third, a cost-based decision threshold, passed on validation and then failed on the test set: it caught no extra fraud and cost 58,800 more than the baseline at 100 units per alert. It is not adopted.
- **One real problem fixed.** The raw-feature model used in the J1 ablation needed 1,886 of its 2,000 allowed trees. With a larger learning rate it needs 733, at the same accuracy.
- **The J1 code is now a tested package.** 45 fast tests and 2 slow ones, all passing. A slow test re-fits the baseline from the package and checks it against the committed J1 numbers.
- **Still broken:** the 4 remaining test misses, and the idea of tuning an operating point from so few errors. Details at the end.

## What was built

The notebook code for loading, features and metrics moved into a `fraud/` package so it can be tested. Notebooks 01 and 02 and every J1 report are unchanged.

The decision rules were written into notebook 03 before any iteration ran: judge iterations on validation only; keep one only if validation PR-AUC rises by more than 2σ, or the expected cost falls without PR-AUC dropping by more than σ; use the test set once at the end with a paired bootstrap. Two clarifications were added before the iterations ran (where the cost is defined, and that a threshold change has to pass a cross-fitted check), and they are marked in the notebook.

Expected cost is the money in the frauds that were not flagged, plus a review cost for every alert. The review cost is an assumption, not something PaySim provides, so results are shown at 10, 100 and 1,000 units per alert.

## Baseline against the final candidate (test set, used once)

| | PR-AUC | Alerts | Missed frauds | False alarms | Missed amount | Cost at 100 per alert |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline (M2.1), max-F1 threshold 0.506 | 0.9972 | 1,228 | 4 | 0 | 822,153 | 944,953 |
| Final candidate (iteration 2), cost threshold 0.041 | 0.9972 | 1,816 | 4 | 588 | 822,153 | 1,003,753 |

The two rows use the same scores, so PR-AUC is identical. The paired bootstrap puts the cost difference at +58,800, 95% interval +54,000 to +63,200, so the candidate is measurably worse, not just unproven. After seeing this I decided not to ship the cost threshold. That decision was made with the test result in view and is recorded that way in notebook 05.

## Iterations (validation set)

| # | Change | Val PR-AUC | Missed | False alarms | Trees | Cost at 100 | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Baseline | 0.99718 | 5 | 0 | 117 | 2,430,870 | reference |
| 1 | Three ratio/flag features | 0.99730 | 5 | 1 | 58 | 2,430,970 | reverted |
| 2 | Threshold chosen by cost instead of F1 | 0.99718 | 3 | 589 | 117 | 1,779,708 | kept on validation, not adopted after test |
| 3 | Second-stage search, 12 configs, 3-fold CV on all of train | 0.99712 | 3 | 1,145 | 78 | 1,835,308 | reverted |

1. **Ratio and flag features.** Before adding them I looked at the five validation misses and at the training set. All five have an emptied origin account but an amount that is not the old balance, and that corner holds 915,854 training rows with 20 frauds (0.002%). I wrote down that I expected little or no gain. PR-AUC moved +0.00012, inside the noise. The model did use the new equality flag (half of the split gain) and came out half the size, but it makes the same errors.
2. **Cost-based threshold.** On validation it caught 2 more frauds for 589 extra false alarms, and cost fell 27% on the rows it was chosen on and 13% when each half was judged with a threshold chosen on the other half. On the test set the saving disappeared (above). The threshold had been decided by two validation transactions.
3. **Second-stage search.** The CV winner (depth 4, learning rate 0.05, min_child_weight 10) beat the baseline configuration in CV, 0.99812 against 0.99681, but not on validation. The search took about 22 minutes of compute for no gain, which matches what J1 found about tuning.

## Problem hit and fixed

**Slow convergence of the raw-feature model.** In J1, the model trained on the six untransformed columns reused the engineered-feature hyper-parameters and stopped at 1,886 trees with a ceiling of 2,000, with its validation curve still creeping up. I defined "converged" as stopping at no more than half the ceiling, tried four settings, and picked on validation.

| | Trees | Seconds in this run | Test PR-AUC | Missed | False alarms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| J1 settings (lr 0.03) | 1,886 | 536 | 0.9289 | 318 | 34 |
| Fixed (lr 0.1) | 733 | 238 | 0.9295 | 317 | 31 |

Accuracy did not change, so the J1 ablation gap (0.93 on raw features against 0.997 with the balance features) is not an under-training artefact. J1's `ablation.csv` is left as committed; the new numbers are in `reports/m2_2/raw_convergence.csv`. The seconds depend on the machine (J1 recorded 258 s for the first row), so only the ratio means anything.

**A second problem, documented rather than fixed:** choosing an operating point from a handful of errors. That is iteration 2 above.

## Tests

`python -m pytest -q` runs 45 tests in about 2 seconds on synthetic frames, so the 470 MB file is not needed. `python -m pytest -m slow` runs 2 more against the real data (about 40 seconds).

- Data: scope keeps only TRANSFER and CASH_OUT and loses no fraud; the split has the right sizes, disjoint and exhaustive parts, the fraud rate in each part, and is deterministic.
- Features: balance errors on hand-built transactions, the hour wrap, the ratio edge case (zero origin balance gives -1, never NaN or inf), and a leakage guard that no label, `isFlaggedFraud`, `step` or identifier column can be an input.
- Metrics: thresholds and confusion counts on toys checked by hand, weighted average precision equal to scikit-learn's (including tied scores and integer weights), and the cost functions including ties and the "flag nothing" case.
- Model and log: hyper-parameters read from the rank-1 row, a smoke test that XGBoost learns a simple rule and stops early, and the log replacing rather than duplicating rows.
- Slow: the package reproduces the J1 split sizes and the J1 test numbers.

One test of mine was wrong, not the code: I expected hour 23 for step 743, and (743 − 1) mod 24 is 22. I fixed the test.

## What is still broken

- **The 4 test misses.** They score 0.0048 to 0.0338, so catching the best one needs 2,486 alerts and the others 64,000 to 378,000. They sit in the corner described under iteration 1, which row-level features do not separate.
- **Choosing the operating point.** Five validation misses are too few to learn a threshold from. Any real operating point should come from a policy or from many more positives.
- **The data.** PaySim is synthetic, so the near-perfect scores say more about how the simulator keeps its books than about real fraud. The review cost is an assumption.
- **Run time.** Notebook 03 takes about 30 minutes, almost all of it iteration 3's search. Notebook 04 takes about 12 minutes and notebook 05 about 3.
- **One seed, one split, one dataset.** The noise floor covers seed variation only.

## Next steps

1. Features from outside the row, such as an account's earlier transactions, to reach the corner the current features cannot.
2. Out-of-fold scores on train plus validation, so a threshold is chosen from thousands of frauds instead of 1,232, or an operating point set by policy.
3. A chronological hold-out for the final model (J1 ran one for the baseline only).
4. The half-size model from iteration 1 is worth revisiting if speed matters; rule 3 does not score speed, so it was reverted.
